International Hiring Framework

A working tool for deciding where 80,000 Hours can — and should — hire outside the UK.

Explanation of the problem

80,000 Hours wants to widen its hiring beyond the UK, but country decisions have historically been made ad hoc. The relevant question is therefore not simply whether Deel can employ someone in a country, but whether hiring there creates a reasonable balance between talent access, mission alignment, collaboration, cost, and operational risk.

This matters because 80,000 Hours is a small, growing organisation: additional employment complexity has a real opportunity cost for the People team, while restricting recruitment geographically could unnecessarily exclude strong candidates.

The framework separates three distinct questions: whether we can hire somewhere, whether it's worth actively recruiting there, and whether an individual candidate can work successfully from there.

In the near term, deeper recruiting efforts could focus on France, Germany, the Netherlands, Argentina, Brazil and South Africa, while treating Chile, Nigeria and Kenya as promising markets to test.

1Hard constraints

Can we hire there at all?

A country should pass four minimum tests. Failure on any one means we generally should not hire there unless we can identify a concrete mitigation.

  • Deel availability — Deel can employ staff there.
  • Regulatory feasibility — no legal or regulatory barrier makes employment impractical.
  • Operational feasibility — sufficiently low compliance, payroll, benefits, tax, and People Ops overhead, in accordance with the team's capacity.
  • Remote-work MVP — candidates can generally perform the job with their own computer and access to sufficiently stable electricity, connectivity, and other essential infrastructure, assuming for the purposes of this exercise that the organization cannot provide every hiree with hardware.
2Soft filters

Is it worth actively recruiting there?

For countries passing Layer 1, assess whether additional recruiting effort is justified. These are search heuristics, not requirements.

  • Talent and mission alignment — evidence of EA, AI-safety, social-impact, university, or adjacent communities, alongside a sufficiently large pool of educated, English-speaking professionals.
  • Collaboration (time zone overlap) — ideally 4–5 hours of overlap with UK working hours, treated as a meaningful cost rather than an absolute barrier, particularly where work can be asynchronous.

Key question: does this country materially expand our probability of finding an excellent candidate relative to the recruiting and coordination costs?

3Individual candidate checks

Can this specific person work successfully from there?

Country-level assessment should generally not substitute for candidate-level assessment — it mitigates country-level risk rather than excluding candidates based on national averages. Mission-alignment itself is always assessed here, at the individual level, regardless of what Layer 2 showed for the country.

  • English and written/async communication
  • Ability to work independently and reliably
  • Actual internet/electricity reliability and backup arrangements
  • Suitable hardware and workspace
  • Sustainable working hours
  • Security/privacy requirements
  • Expected compensation and total employment cost

General costs and benefits (SWOT)

↑ Strengths

Larger talent pool; greater mission-aligned talent access; potential compensation efficiency; geographic diversification; reduced dependence on European labour markets.

↓ Weaknesses

More People Ops complexity; recruitment/screening costs; timezone friction; variable infrastructure; greater compensation benchmarking complexity.

◇ Opportunities

Build repeatable international hiring pipelines; learn which countries produce strong hires; develop more asynchronous ways of working; access emerging AI-safety ecosystems.

! Threats

Employment-law changes; FX volatility; connectivity/power disruption; coordination costs; overestimating the depth of a mission-aligned talent pool based on a small community.

Recommended countries for the future

If a main goal is to prevent geography from being a barrier, we distinguish countries where we should actively source from countries where we should simply be comfortable hiring an excellent candidate if one turns up.

Active recruiting now

France · Germany · Netherlands · Argentina · Brazil · South Africa

Promising — treat as a test

Chile · Nigeria · Kenya

Future — once async infrastructure matures

India · Philippines · Singapore · Australia · New Zealand

Our existing experience hiring in North America is a useful pilot for testing collaboration across larger time differences.

The longer-term goal is to expand the search wherever the marginal talent-access benefit exceeds the additional operational and collaboration cost, rather than establishing permanent geographic exclusions.
